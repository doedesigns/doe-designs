A CONCEPTUAL NONPROFIT BRAND & WEBSITE

Project Overview

Turning environmental awareness into meaningful action.

Clean Future Network is a conceptual environmental nonprofit created to make nature conservation and community cleanup more approachable. The website brings together environmental education, sustainability resources, and opportunities for people to connect and take action.

Inspired by the principles of Leave No Trace, the project explores how a welcoming digital experience can help people understand their impact on the environment and discover practical ways to make a difference.

DESIGNED FOR PEOPLE AT EVERY STAGE OF INVOLVEMENT

Who Is It For?

  • The curious beginner: People who want to help protect the environment but aren't sure where to start.
  • The new community member: People who have moved to a new area and want to find local initiatives and like-minded individuals.
  • The experienced advocate: People already involved in environmental cleanup who want to share knowledge, resources, and opportunities.

PURPOSE MEETS PRACTICALITY

Making Environmental Action More Accessible

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Make environmental information actionable.

Environmental awareness doesn't always translate into action. People may want to help but lack the information, resources, or community connections to know where to begin.

  • Organize educational resources, news, and events.
  • Make information easy to scan and navigate.
  • Connect visitors with meaningful ways to participate.
02 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Create a welcoming place to get involved.

Create a welcoming digital resource that makes environmental education easier to explore, connects people with practical opportunities, and encourages community participation.

  • Establish a consistent information architecture.
  • Use reusable layouts and a clear content hierarchy.
  • Explore multilingual access and community-focused content.

RESEARCH, INSPIRATION & DISCOVERY

From Research to Design

EXPLORING EXISTING EXPERIENCES

I began by reviewing a range of websites, including environmental and nature-focused nonprofits, to explore how they organized information, presented educational content, and encouraged community involvement.

I also looked beyond the environmental space for interaction ideas. One website featured a dynamic comment area populated by visitor contributions. I liked the idea of bringing that sense of community into Clean Future Network and adapting it to the nonprofit's purpose.

WHAT I TOOK FORWARD

  • Clear organization of educational resources and community information.
  • A natural visual language using greens, browns, and warm orange tones.
  • Opportunities for visitors to participate rather than simply consume information.
  • Interactive elements that give the website personality and encourage exploration.

DESIGNING FOR DISCOVERY & CONNECTION

Small Interactions, More Personality

I wanted the website to feel more engaging than a collection of informational pages. Two custom features bring movement and a sense of community into the experience.

01 / ROTATING PAGE HEADLINES

Each page introduces its content with a changing headline. A page-specific phrase, such as "For the Love of:" or "Make Contact With:", is followed by an automatically cycling series of words: People, The World, Nature, Safety, Health, Yourself, and The Future.

The changing language reinforces the idea that environmental responsibility connects to many aspects of everyday life, while adding movement and encouraging visitors to pause and explore.

02 / COMMUNITY COMMENTS

Visitors can submit comments through the Contact page and choose to have their contributions appear in the homepage comment panel.

Comments appear and disappear at random, creating a changing snapshot of community participation. The vertical peach panel adds a warm visual contrast and gives the experience a more personal, welcoming quality.

STRUCTURE · IDENTITY · APPLICATION

From First Sketch to Finished Experience

01 / STRUCTURE

Plan the Experience

I translated the initial concept into page sketches and a low-fidelity layout, establishing the content hierarchy, navigation, and primary sections before moving into Figma.

02 / IDENTITY

Define the Visual Language

I developed a nature-inspired palette of greens, browns, and warm orange tones, pairing imagery and typography to create a calm, approachable visual identity.

03 / APPLICATION

Bring It to Life

I refined the designs in Figma and brought the website to life in Dreamweaver, building the page layouts and adding custom interactions to support the experience.

Image selection was an important part of the visual direction. I sourced licensed imagery through Envato Elements, choosing photographs that reinforced the purpose of each page—from community cleanup and environmental education to nature and sustainability.

BRINGING THE CONCEPT TO LIFE

Beyond the Classroom

I wanted to create a website that felt like a polished, usable experience rather than a typical classroom assignment. While the course introduced the design process and website development, I independently explored additional techniques to bring my ideas to life.

Using Dreamweaver, I built the website and challenged myself to implement more advanced features, including the rotating page headlines, automatically cycling imagery, and layered footer composition. Because JavaScript wasn't covered in the course, I independently learned the fundamentals needed to build the interactive headline behavior.

This process pushed me beyond the original assignment and gave me an opportunity to explore the relationship between visual design, interaction, and front-end implementation.

VALIDATION & REFLECTION

Review & Next Steps

I personally checked the navigation and links throughout the coded website. Although the project was available for classroom review, formal usability testing with representative visitors was outside the scope of the assignment.

Given additional time, I would test how easily first-time visitors locate environmental resources, understand the rotating headlines, and discover opportunities to participate. I would also evaluate whether the community comment feature encourages meaningful engagement.

Project Takeaways

This project brought together brand identity, information architecture, UX/UI design, and website development. It strengthened my ability to turn a broad concept into a cohesive digital experience while independently exploring new technical skills to support the design.
