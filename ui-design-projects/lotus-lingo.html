A CONCEPTUAL LANGUAGE-LEARNING APPコンセプトの語学学習アプリ

PROJECT OVERVIEWプロジェクト概要

Lotus Lingo is a conceptual Japanese-learning app shaped by my own experience teaching myself the language with several different learning tools. I wanted to bring the most useful parts of that experience into one place while addressing a few recurring frustrations: unclear lesson progression, repetitive practice, and limited ways to use a language beyond the lesson itself.

The concept connects guided lessons with writing and speaking practice, games, music discovery, and community spaces. Its structure is designed to help learners understand what to do next while still giving them room to choose how they practice. I developed more than 100 high-fidelity screens to explore the product across onboarding, learning, motivation, and social features.

THE DESIGN OPPORTUNITYデザインの課題

CHALLENGE & SOLUTION課題と解決策

01

THE CHALLENGE

Help learners build momentum without making the experience feel repetitive.

Learning Japanese involves more than memorizing vocabulary. Learners move between different writing systems, written and spoken practice, and formal and informal language. They also need a clear sense of progression and reasons to return between lessons.

02

THE SOLUTION

Connect structured learning with meaningful ways to practice.

Lotus Lingo pairs a guided lesson sequence with distinct writing and speaking activities, games, rewards, music discovery, and level-based community rooms. The goal is to make the next step easy to find while offering different ways to reinforce what learners are studying.

Visual identity / Design system

COLOR, TYPE & COMPONENTS色・文字・コンポーネント

CREATIVE DIRECTIONクリエイティブディレクション

The visual identity uses a bright palette of greens, deep pink, light blue, and soft peach, balanced with friendly typography and original illustrations. I created the lotus, cherry blossoms, torii gates, profile artwork, and navigation icons to give the product a recognizable visual language rather than relying entirely on generic interface elements.

The interface also needed to make different kinds of Japanese practice feel distinct. Reading and writing activities, pronunciation and speaking exercises, and formal versus informal language each have different learning needs. The Katakana lesson screens show how explanations, character recognition, pronunciation support, and writing practice can be organized into a more guided sequence.

DESIGNING FOR THE LEARNER学習者のためのデザイン

USER JOURNEYユーザージャーニー

This proposed journey maps the experience from first-time setup to ongoing practice. It helped me consider what learners may need at each stage, where they could lose momentum, and how the app's features might support a return visit.

01 / GETTING STARTED

Discover & Begin

USER GOAL

Understand the app and find a comfortable starting point for learning Japanese.

POSSIBLE FRICTION

Too much initial information or an unclear first step could make getting started feel overwhelming.

02 / LEARNING

Learn & Practice

USER GOAL

Build familiarity through structured lessons, interactive games, and varied practice activities.

POSSIBLE FRICTION

This journey represents design hypotheses, not findings from formal user research or usability testing.

RESEARCH & INFORMATION ARCHITECTUREリサーチと情報設計

FROM RESEARCH TO DESIGNリサーチからデザインへ

I approached this project as an active Japanese learner, drawing on my experience with several language-learning apps. I noticed that the apps that gave me reasons to return every day were not necessarily the ones that helped me learn most effectively. I wanted to bring those two strengths together: an experience that encourages consistent engagement while making meaningful learning progress. I also found it difficult to understand how hiragana, katakana, and kanji fit together and how to approach learning each writing system. The apps I used didn't fully address that challenge from my perspective.

These observations shaped the product's learning priorities: a clearer lesson sequence, distinct writing and speaking activities, and ways to reinforce learning beyond the core lesson. I also considered how learners could move between related features without losing their place. A detailed hamburger menu provides access to the app's broader structure, while contextual links create additional routes between related screens. This gives users more than one way to find their next destination.

App concept map / Information architecture

I mapped the app's structure and developed a high-fidelity Figma prototype with more than 100 screens spanning onboarding, lessons, games, music discovery, community features, rewards, and account management. This was an experience-led academic concept, not a formal user research study or a validated product. The next step would be to test the navigation and learning flows with Japanese learners and use their feedback to refine the experience.

The product concept extends beyond the lesson itself. Learners can practice through games, explore Japanese music, connect with others, and see how their activity contributes to their progress.

COMMUNITY & CONVERSATION

Premium chat rooms are organized around learner levels and shared interests, with topics such as animals, anime, fashion, food, and music. The concept also explores career- and location-based conversation spaces to give learners more relevant ways to connect and practice.

MUSIC & CULTURAL DISCOVERY

A curated Japanese music area introduces artists and album-inspired selections as another way to engage with the language. English spellings are included to make artists easier to search for outside the app. This is a static concept in the prototype, not a live music integration.

PROGRESS & MOTIVATION

The profile and stats area is designed as a destination for lesson progress and earned awards, with the intention that these indicators update as learners use the app. The prototype illustrates the intended interface; it does not store activity or automatically update a learner's progress.

WHAT I WOULD REFINE NEXT次に改善したいこと

DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS & NEXT STEPS設計上の検討事項と次のステップ

Make progress persistent. The stats screen was designed to reflect completed lessons and earned awards, but the prototype does not save learner activity. A future version would need persistent progress data and a thoughtful way to help returning learners pick up where they left off, while still allowing them to choose a different lesson.

Validate the learning flows. The project was not tested through formal usability sessions. I would next test onboarding, lesson progression, and the distinction between writing, speaking, and language-register activities with Japanese learners at different experience levels.

Connect the concept to real services and future content. Music discovery currently uses curated static content, and the additional language options are an exploration of future expansion rather than completed language experiences. A production version would need to validate content partnerships, build out each language's learning structure, and test the community features with users.
